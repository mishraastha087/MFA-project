About MFA

What does Multi-Factor Authentication mean?

Multi-Factor Authentication is a security process that requires you to verify your identity using two or more independent factors before you can access an account or system. It goes beyond a single password, combining knowledge, possession, and biometric checks to confirm that you really are who you claim to be.

Why MFA is important

Passwords are easy to guess, reuse, or steal through phishing and data leaks. MFA adds a second layer of defense, so a leaked password alone is not enough for an attacker to break in. This makes it far harder for cybercriminals to access personal, financial, or organizational data.

Because of this, MFA is now recommended — and often required — by banks, email providers, workplaces, and government portals as a baseline security standard.

Advantages & Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Significantly reduces the risk of unauthorized access.
  • Protects accounts even if a password is stolen.
  • Builds user trust in online platforms and services.
  • Helps organizations meet security compliance standards.
  • Works well alongside other security practices.

Disadvantages

  • Adds an extra step to the login process.
  • Requires access to a phone, email, or security key.
  • Users may be locked out if they lose their second factor.
  • Some methods, like SMS OTP, can be intercepted.
  • May need initial setup and user education.
