Authentication Factors

The three main authentication factors

MFA combines proofs from at least two of the categories below. Understanding each one helps explain why combining them is so effective.

1. Something You Know

This factor relies on private information stored in your memory — like a password, PIN, or the answer to a security question. It's the most common factor, but also the easiest to guess, reuse, or leak, which is why it's rarely used alone.

Quick Facts

Examples: Passwords · PINs · Security questions
Weakness: Can be guessed, phished, or leaked

2. Something You Have

This factor relies on a physical or digital item that only you possess — such as your smartphone, a one-time password (OTP), or a hardware security key. Because an attacker would need the actual device, this factor is much harder to compromise remotely.

Quick Facts

Examples: OTP codes · Security keys · Authenticator apps
Strength: Requires physical access to bypass

3. Something You Are

This factor uses a unique biometric trait to confirm identity, such as a fingerprint, facial scan, or voice pattern. It's convenient and hard to replicate, though it usually requires dedicated hardware like a sensor or camera.

Quick Facts

Examples: Fingerprint · Face ID · Voice recognition
Strength: Unique to each individual
